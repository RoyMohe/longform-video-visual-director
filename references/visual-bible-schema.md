# Account Visual Bible Schema

Use this schema when establishing or revising the stable channel system. Adapt detail to the user's needs; do not fill sections with generic adjectives.

## 1. Strategic core

- Channel promise
- Audience and viewing context
- Narrator persona: three traits plus explicit non-traits
- Desired viewer feeling before, during, and after an episode
- Topic range and typical episode duration
- Production model: presenter, team, tools, cadence, recurring budget

## 2. Visual thesis

Write one sentence connecting the editorial promise to a screen behavior. Then define three to five design principles and three anti-principles.

Example form: “We make invisible systems feel inspectable by turning every claim into a layered piece of evidence the viewer can open.” Do not reuse this wording unless it truly fits.

## 3. Signature system

Define at least three recognizable devices in different layers: a compositional signature, a typographic or annotation signature, and a motion, transition, evidence, sound, or host-integration signature.

For each device specify its narrative purpose, construction, suitable moments, frequency, and misuse to avoid.

## 4. Core visual language

- Palette with roles and practical color values; include light/dark and accessibility behavior
- Typography by role, fallback strategy, numeral and bilingual behavior
- Grid, safe zones, margins, alignment, density, and aspect-ratio adaptation
- Shape, line, icon, texture, depth, lighting, shadow, and image-treatment rules
- Presenter framing, wardrobe/background relationship, eye line, and compositing rules
- Documentary evidence: documents, quotes, screenshots, citations, archive, and data
- Illustration, diagram, UI, product, 3D, and generated-image rules as relevant

Do not invent licensed font availability. If fonts are unknown, define characteristics and list realistic candidates for later confirmation.

## 5. Motion and edit grammar

- Baseline edit rhythm and allowed variation
- Entry/exit behaviors
- Camera and parallax rules
- Transition family with narrative meanings
- Chapter opener, reset, comparison, reveal, correction, and ending behaviors
- Motion curves or timing only when useful to the production team
- Practices to avoid

## 6. Sound language

- Music role and energy curve
- Recurring sonic motif
- Interface/annotation/transition cues
- Ambience and silence rules
- Relationship between sound accents and visual motion

## 7. Recurring templates

Define only reusable templates the channel will actually need, such as cold open/title, chapter card, evidence/document inspection, statistic/chart, timeline, comparison, system map, quote, definition, presenter plus graphic, correction/uncertainty, ending callback, and source card.

For each template state input, layout logic, motion logic, and when not to use it.

## 8. Episode extension rules

Clarify what stays fixed and what may vary by topic:

- locked core: identity-critical rules;
- controlled variables: episode accent, motif, archive era, illustration metaphor;
- free layer: unique hero moments that still obey hierarchy and evidence rules.

## 9. Production and governance

- L1/L2/L3 production capabilities and expected mix
- Asset sourcing, naming, citation, licensing, and versioning conventions
- Approval checkpoints
- What qualifies as a visual-bible change versus an episode exception
- A short pre-export checklist

End with a one-screen quick reference containing the rules an editor needs every day.
