# Directing Principles

Use these principles to make editorial creator videos coherent, credible, and producible.

## Abstract reference material instead of cloning it

Analyze references along independent dimensions:

- editorial stance: investigative, explanatory, skeptical, playful, intimate, cinematic;
- information architecture: headline-first, evidence-first, narrative reveal, comparison, timeline, system map;
- picture sources: host, location, archive, documents, UI, products, charts, diagrams, illustration, 3D, generated imagery;
- graphic surface: flat, paper, glass, terminal, print, newsroom, technical drawing, tactile object;
- typography: family, scale contrast, density, alignment, annotation style, number treatment;
- composition: canvas, grid, margins, panels, depth, negative space, host placement;
- motion: entrance logic, camera behavior, object choreography, transition family, tempo;
- sound: narration relationship, interface cues, impacts, ambiences, motif, silence.

Reference boards of premium Chinese knowledge and business channels often share strong hierarchy, restrained backgrounds, a small accent palette, evidence-rich screens, presenter resets, charts/UI/documents, and designed transitions. These are category conventions, not a complete identity. Build originality through a distinctive combination of editorial promise, visual metaphor family, compositional rule, type behavior, and motion/sound signature.

## Design from meaning

Every shot should primarily do one job:

- **Anchor:** establish trust, personality, location, or a new chapter through the presenter.
- **Prove:** show a source, quote, product, interface, data point, or observable event.
- **Explain:** reveal structure, causality, scale, sequence, comparison, or mechanism.
- **Evoke:** create emotional context or a memorable metaphor without pretending to be evidence.
- **Orient:** communicate chapter, time, place, actor, or transition.
- **Release:** lower information density so viewers can absorb the previous point.

If a shot has no clear job, remove or redesign it. Do not illustrate every noun. Prefer showing relationships, stakes, change, and contradiction.

## Evidence contract

- Distinguish real source material, accurate recreation, conceptual illustration, and generated imagery in production notes.
- Do not fabricate screenshots, headlines, citations, product behavior, or data visualizations.
- If a required asset is unknown, mark a research placeholder with the exact retrieval need.
- For charts, name the metric, unit, time span, source requirement, and intended takeaway.
- For quotes and documents, specify the exact excerpt to highlight and keep surrounding context available to the editor.
- Treat generative imagery as illustration unless it accurately reconstructs a documented visual and is labeled accordingly.

## Rhythm and attention

Cut on a change in idea, evidence, perspective, scale, or emotion—not on a fixed timer. Use micro-beats inside dense explanations and longer holds for consequence, wonder, tension, or reflection.

Alternate cognitive loads. A dense data or document screen should often be followed by a presenter, a simple diagram, a tactile object, or atmospheric relief. Re-anchor the viewer at chapter boundaries and after long abstract passages.

Use visual callbacks. Establish a motif early, complicate it through the argument, and resolve or transform it near the ending. Avoid using the signature transition between every shot; scarcity makes it recognizable.

## Screen text

- Screen text should label, compare, quantify, quote, or sharpen—not duplicate whole narration sentences.
- Establish three or fewer recurring hierarchy levels for ordinary episodes.
- Make the reading order unambiguous and give viewers enough dwell time.
- Preserve exact wording for sourced quotes and visible UI. Clearly label paraphrases.
- Define number, unit, date, citation, and bilingual-text behavior in the channel bible.

## Production realism

Match ambition to recurring capacity. Use a simple production-tier notation when helpful:

- **L1 Editorial:** existing footage, screen capture, documents, photos, type, simple charts.
- **L2 Designed:** custom 2D diagrams, tracked composites, multi-layer transitions, controlled shoots.
- **L3 Hero:** bespoke 3D, location production, complex simulations, extensive generated sequences.

Reserve L3 for a few story-critical moments. A repeatable channel identity should survive an L1 episode.

## Audit dimensions

Score or discuss only what is useful: identity recognition without logo, hierarchy, narration-to-picture logic, evidence credibility, host/B-roll integration, motion consistency, cognitive load, production repeatability, topic adaptability, and originality relative to references.
