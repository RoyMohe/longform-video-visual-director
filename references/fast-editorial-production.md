# Fast Editorial Production

Use this profile when the user wants short-video grammar, rapid delivery, no presenter, or a source-heavy reference style.

## First principle

Narration length and visual grammar are independent. A 20–30 minute essay may still use brisk short-video editing. Historical, philosophical, or speculative writing does not justify documentary spectacle unless the user explicitly budgets for it.

## Default no-presenter mix

- 55–65% real or licensed footage.
- 15–20% webpages, documents, photos, interfaces, and evidence.
- 10–15% reusable packaging templates.
- 5–10% simple custom 2D diagrams.
- 0–3% hero composites; at most one every 60–120 seconds.

Call this the 80/20 rule: roughly 80% sourced material and 20% packaging. A missing presenter is normally replaced by more evidence and stronger editorial selection, not more bespoke animation.

## Rhythm

- Hook, 0–15 seconds: refresh information every 0.8–2 seconds.
- Body source shots: usually 2–5 seconds.
- Documents and charts: 5–8 seconds, with internal crop, highlight, cursor, or focus changes.
- Static holds over 8 seconds are exceptional.
- One narration sentence group normally uses 2–4 source shots.
- Add one light packaging action every 8–15 seconds.
- Use one complete reusable template every 20–40 seconds.

Do not cut mechanically on a fixed interval. Refresh when meaning, evidence, focus, or emotional pressure changes.

## Source-first hierarchy

Choose the cheapest truthful layer that communicates the point:

1. Exact evidence: webpage, document, interface, chart, quote, or archival source.
2. Concrete reality: people, places, products, work, institutions, or events.
3. Editorial organization: crop, split screen, annotation, caption, timeline, or comparison.
4. Simple 2D explanation: arrows, nodes, steps, paths, and quantities.
5. Hero composite only for the opening thesis, central reversal, or ending payoff.

Avoid full-time 3D worlds, particle systems, cinematic environments, unique transition designs, and object-by-object sound effects unless specifically requested.

## Repeatable premium packaging

Premium means disciplined selection and hierarchy:

- consistent source frame and citation strip;
- one subtitle hierarchy;
- no more than one accent color per screen;
- reusable viewpoint cards, definition cards, comparison cards, and chapter cards;
- source labels and uncertainty labels such as `资料画面`, `示意`, or `视觉类比`;
- one music bed plus a small fixed set of transition and emphasis sounds.

## Reference calibration workflow

When the user supplies videos:

1. Sample the full runtime rather than judging the opening only.
2. Estimate source footage, presenter, documents, and custom graphics separately.
3. Measure visual-refresh intervals when tools permit; distinguish visual refresh from full scene replacement.
4. Derive ranges and reusable rules, never imitate a creator's signature frame for frame.
5. Produce a 60–90 second pilot using the user's narration.
6. Ask for approval of density, material mix, and packaging before scripting the whole episode.

## Script granularity

Each row should be a 3–8 second edit unit. Include:

- exact narration span;
- 1–3 concrete source shots or one readable evidence view;
- the editorial operation, such as hard cut, crop, highlight, match cut, or split screen;
- minimal screen text;
- source/acquisition category;
- any factual or licensing label.

The row is an instruction to an editor, not a pitch to a film crew.
